text = "One time, I went on an archaeological expedition, where I learned firsthand that all the hidden stories of antiquity are essentially hours and days of excavation, measuring, and clearing dust under the scorching sun. And someday, you’ll understand what objects you’ve found and what their purpose was. Photographic “interdisciplinary vision” became a record of my sensations from what I saw in the past and present, from images that attest to both past existence and temporal distance. Everything became an expedition document: artifact inventories, ceramic heaps, terrain views, the daily life, labor, and triumph of the participants. The art of photography finally became for me the deformation of the ordinary into the mysterious and multilayered, accompanied, when necessary, by scientific explanation."

[[extra.blocks]]

This experience truly enriched my understanding of how photography can be a powerful tool for studying civilizational heritage, both living and man-made. Before it meets the fate of Palmyra…"

type = "h2"
text = "The Infrared Turn"

[[extra.blocks]]
type = "p"
text = "After years of documenting the world, I became captivated by the idea of challenging our ways of seeing. Berger taught us that any perspective is a construct. What if I could use a technology that offered a perspective entirely alien to the human eye?"

[[extra.blocks]]
type = "p"
text = "The infrared turn occurred with complex landscapes and shifted to human history in the invisible world. The visible light portrait of a person created (in Barthes’s terminology) a studium — a culturally conditioned interest in the image. But the reflection of faces, skin, clothing, iron, and walls in infrared light created a punctum — an unintended detail that establishes a connection with the “old world” of psychedelics, rock ’n’ roll, and Kodak Aerochrome."

[[extra.blocks]]
type = "p"
text = "Archaeological landscapes in invisible light became a reflection of the history of the human past. Civilizational heritage, with profound philosophical implications, easily transforms into picturesque piles of stones against a backdrop of endless international considerations."

Then came 590nm with its false colors: orange leaves, cyan skies, the full “super color” experience. And finally IR Chrome — digital recreation of Kodak Aerochrome, the military surveillance film from Vietnam era that turned jungles pink and made camouflage visible."

type = "h2"
text = "Modus Ex Machina"

[[extra.blocks]]
type = "p"
text = "In 2021, at the European University in St. Petersburg, we combined art history with machine learning technologies to create the “Art and Artificial Intelligence” lab. A documentary photographer and art historian with a love of adventure became its co-founder and CTO."

[[extra.blocks]]
type = "p"
text = "Our main project was a neural network proudly named SIMILIS — “Shazam for Pottery Sherds” — that searches for analogies with archaeological finds based on photographs. The first attempt was comical: the most “similar” matches the machine found were a button and a cannonball. But after dozens of iterations, the accuracy of the comparisons increased."

Discussing the meanings of “reproduction,” “analogy,” “accuracy,” and “digital compromise,” we found the neural network not a replacement for every expert, but a partner in a new kind of dialogue. Between the rigorous transistor mind and the soul of the historian, only photography will serve as a common language."

[[extra.blocks]]

Mathematical beauty cannot replace photographic humanism. I suspect that this database and seven neural networks are the most useful things I will leave behind (besides my children and my archive)."

[[extra.blocks]]

text = "All my life, I sought the invisible — in the layers of history, in the spectra of light. In May 2025, the invisible found me. The invisible hand of the Russian state security service upended my life in a single day. I had to shut down my lab projects, leave the country, and master the simple craft of a “digital nomad” and “independent researcher.” No one in Russia of 2025 is safe from arbitrariness, violence, terror, and threats from the state — I prefer to think I got off easy for the so-called “discreditation of the army and authorities.”"

And, of course, to continue shooting the stories of people in natural and infrared light. [[extra.blocks]]
type = "p"
text = "I work with full-spectrum converted cameras and a range of infrared filters. Each filter reveals a different aspect of the invisible world: 590nm creates false-color imagery with vivid foliage shifts, 720nm produces dramatic black-and-white photographs, and IR Chrome digitally recreates the false-color aesthetic of Kodak Aerochrome film directly in-camera."
